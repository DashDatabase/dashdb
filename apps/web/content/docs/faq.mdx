## Is DashDB free?

Yes. The editor is free, with no signup and no limits on projects or tables. Cloud sync and
team features are planned as paid plans; the local editor stays free. See [Pricing](/pricing).

## Where are my schemas stored?

In your browser, on your device (IndexedDB). DashDB has no server-side copy of your projects
and never uploads them. Download a [project file](/docs/importing/project-files) to back one
up or move it to another machine.

## Does DashDB connect to my database?

No. To import a live schema you run a read-only query in your own SQL client and paste the
result ([how](/docs/importing/existing-database)). Migrations are files you run yourself.

## Which databases are supported?

PostgreSQL and MySQL 8, for design, import and migrations. Prisma schemas and DBML import into
either, depending on their `provider` or `database_type`.

## Does DashDB run migrations for me?

No, by design. DashDB writes and reviews the migration; your existing tooling (Prisma Migrate,
drizzle-kit, Flyway, `psql` and so on) runs it, in the same pipeline as the rest of your
deploy. See [Using DashDB with your ORM](/docs/orms).

## How reliable are the safety rules?

They encode how PostgreSQL (11 and later) and MySQL 8 take locks and rewrite tables, and every
rule is tested against generated SQL. They can't know your traffic or table sizes, so read
each finding with your largest tables in mind. A clean review means DashDB found nothing
risky, not that a migration needs no thought. See [Safety rules](/docs/safety-rules).

## Can my team work on the same schema?

Today, share a project file: commit the `.dashdb.json` next to your migrations, and anyone can
import it. Real-time collaboration and cloud sync are planned.

## Can I use DashDB on a phone or tablet?

On screens narrower than 1024 pixels the editor shows a read-only outline of the schema. Use a
laptop or desktop to edit.

## Is DashDB open source?

DashDB's code is public under the Functional Source License (FSL-1.1-MIT): you can read, run,
modify and self-host it for anything except a competing commercial product, and each release
becomes MIT-licensed two years after it ships. That makes it "fair source" rather than open
source in the OSI sense. See [Source code](/open-source).

## Can I run DashDB on my own server?

Yes. It is a Node.js web app with no database of its own. See [Self-hosting](/docs/self-hosting).

## I'm moving from dbdiagram.io. Can I bring my diagrams?

Yes. Export them as DBML and [import](/docs/importing/dbml) them. You keep the structure and
gain migrations and safety checks.
